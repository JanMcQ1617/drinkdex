# My Bar, shelves

9 Oct 2026. For Jan's note: "make my bar section way more organized, its difficult for me to use and I created it with you, so make it more easy to use". It also answers his earlier note the same day, "I want less stuff. it looks really clumped up and a lot of information". It stays inside the less-stuff budget in `../../layout/spec.md` and uses the v4 Daylight palette as `../../white-min/` applies it.

**The model in one line.** What you have is one inventory, grouped by type into seven shelves that are always in the same order, A to Z inside each shelf. Nothing on the screen is ranked, so nothing ever moves. "You can make 31 ›" is the payoff at the top, and one line under it names the next bottle.

Files:

- `index.html`: nine frames at 440 × 956. Add `#empty`, `#basics`, `#stocked`, `#picker`, `#search`, `#ingredient`, `#removed`, `#next` or `#make` to the URL to show one frame.
- `contact.png`: the wall at 1x. Plus one PNG per frame at 2x (880 × 1912): `empty.png`, `basics.png`, `stocked.png`, `picker.png`, `search.png`, `ingredient.png`, `removed.png`, `next.png`, `make.png`.
- `data.js`: every number and name the frames show. It is generated by `tools/data.py` from `src/data/barIndex.json` and `src/data/drinks.json`.
- `tools/match.py`: `matchBar` ported line for line from `src/lib/bar.ts`, with the family fix in section 7.4 applied. `tools/shelves.py`: the proposed shelf and family of all 490 ingredients. `tools/prints.py`: daylight thumbs, made with `../../white/tools/daylight.py`'s own bake. `tools/render.sh`: renders the PNGs using a throwaway headless Chrome profile in this folder. It kills only processes carrying that profile path, then deletes the profile.

The stocked bar in the frames holds these 12: gin, white rum, bourbon, sweet vermouth, Campari, triple sec, Angostura bitters, lemon, lime, orange, sugar syrup and soda water. Together they make **31**, and every number in the frames is worked out from that bar.

## 1. What it fixes

| Audit problem (high) | How shelves answers it |
|---|---|
| The checklist is ranked by "unlocks most right now". Its order depends on what you own, it is re-ranked on every visit, and it carries rank numerals | Nothing is ranked. Your inventory is in shelf order, A to Z. Each picker uses a fixed order taken from the data (how many recipes name an ingredient), which is the same for everyone on every visit. There are no numerals. |
| Rows reshuffle between visits, and paging resets | There is no snapshot and no paging. A list that is not ranked has nothing to re-sort, and every list scrolls to its end. The only frozen order left is the Next bottle sheet, which holds its order while it is open (5.7). |
| What I have and what I could add are mixed in one list | They live on two surfaces. The screen shows only what you have. Adding happens in a picker sheet, where ticked and unticked rows sit in fixed places. |
| The "In your bar" view lies (an unticked item stays listed, an Add is missing) | The inventory always matches the store exactly. Changes land while a sheet covers the screen, or right after one closes. A removal comes with a toast and Undo. |
| 490 ingredients in one flat list with no structure | There are seven shelves, and each one has its own picker. The 490 split into Spirits 100, Liqueurs & vermouth 109, Mixers 80, Fresh 51, Syrups & sugar 37, Bitters 9 and Other 104. A whole shelf scrolls with no "Show more". Spirits are grouped by family inside their picker (4.3). |
| The payoff (the drinks I can make) sits below the whole picker | "You can make 31 ›" is the first thing under the title. While you tick, the picker's foot shows the live total, in view. |

The medium-severity problems are answered too. The 15 chips are gone. The empty "Everything else" chip is gone: Other shows only what you own. Vermouth now sits with the liqueurs. Each row's "+N" shows what a tick would do for you. The basics get a review step. Removing something has an obvious home. The Brass chrome is gone.

## 4. The shelves and the order rules

### 4.1 Seven shelves, always in this order

| Shelf | From the index's categories | Notable moves |
|---|---|---|
| Spirits | `spirit` | Sloe gin, apricot brandy, peach brandy, cherry brandy and coconut rum move to the liqueurs. |
| Liqueurs & vermouth | `liqueur`, plus `wine` that is fortified or aromatised (vermouth, sherry, port, Lillet-style) | Allspice dram and Cherry Heering join. |
| Mixers | `mixer`, `juice`, plus `wine` you top or lengthen with (Champagne, Prosecco, still wine, beer, cider, sake) | Lemonade and grapefruit soda (filed as citrus) join. |
| Fresh | `citrus`, `produce`, `dairy` | Your fridge and fruit bowl: lemon, lime, mint, egg white, cream. |
| Syrups & sugar | `syrup` | Lime cordial, cinnamon syrup, vanilla syrup and the shrubs join. |
| Bitters | `bitters` | The stray "Angostura" (`other`) merges into Angostura bitters (7.4). |
| Other | `spice`, `savoury`, `other` | Nutmeg, cinnamon, Worcestershire, hot sauce, and the long tail. |

The rule is the category plus about 30 per-id overrides (`tools/shelves.py` holds the list). `build-bar-index.mjs` writes the result as a `shelf` field, so the app never guesses.

Why this order: it is the order a bar is stocked and a recipe is read. Spirit first, then modifier, then lengthener, then fresh, then sweet, then dashes, then the rest. All seven always show, so a shelf is in the same place on day one and on day three hundred, and every shelf keeps its own Add.

### 4.2 Inside a shelf on the screen: A to Z

Labels are compared case- and accent-folded (the existing `fold`), so Crème de cacao sorts among the Cs. Nothing else enters the sort: not use, not reach, not gain, not when you added it. Adding Dry vermouth puts it between Campari and Sweet vermouth, the same place every time.

### 4.3 Inside a picker: a fixed order from the data

- **Liqueurs & vermouth, Mixers, Fresh, Syrups & sugar, Bitters, Other.** The order is how many recipes name the ingredient (`uses`, from the index, not from your bar), then A to Z. The common bottles come first (Sweet vermouth 76, Dry vermouth 70, Maraschino 44, Triple sec 39 …) and the long tail comes last. It is static data, worked out once per app version, so a row is where it was yesterday.
- **Spirits.** The same rule, inside families that are kept together, in this order: Gin, Rum, Whiskey, Brandy, Tequila & mezcal, Vodka, More spirits. Each family has a small `muted` head. The families come from the index's own family slots (the slot that a generic like "rum" expands to). This puts White rum, Dark rum and Gold rum next to one another instead of scattered between Vodka and Bourbon.
- **Ticking never moves a row.** The box fills or empties where it stands. Owned rows are not pulled to the top.

### 4.4 Search

The same matcher runs from the top-bar glyph (All ingredients) and from inside any shelf's picker. A shelf picker's field searches everything, because you should not have to know the shelf first.

- **What matches:** the label, its **aliases**, and its **family or shelf words**. Aliases are the left sides of `build-bar-index.mjs`'s VARIANT map, which already folds "Cointreau", "Orange liqueur", "Simple syrup", "Light rum" and "Lime juice" into one bottle. Until now they were thrown away after the build; now they are emitted per ingredient. Family words: "whiskey", "whisky", "rum", "gin", "brandy" and "tequila" list their family. Shelf words: "citrus", "juice", "syrup", "bitters", "vermouth" and "wine" list the matching shelf or group. A small brand list sits on top (Martini Rosso → sweet vermouth, Noilly Prat → dry vermouth, Bacardi → white rum, Kahlúa → coffee liqueur, Baileys → Irish cream, Luxardo → maraschino, club soda or seltzer → soda water).
- **Order:** label starts with the query, then alias starts with it, then family or shelf word, then contains. Ties go to `uses`, then A to Z. The cap of 40 stays.
- **What a row says:** the canonical label. A small `muted` line shows only when it explains the hit: the alias that matched ("Cointreau" under Triple sec), a covered generic ("Any whiskey · covered by your bourbon"), or, inside a shelf picker, the other shelf a hit lives on ("On Spirits").
- **No match:** "Nothing called “xyz”. Try a kind, like rum or citrus." in body `muted`.

### 4.5 The Make list: by base spirit, A to Z

Sections follow the Spirits picker's family order, then **Without a spirit** (Americano, Garibaldi, Milano-Torino). A drink's base is the family of the first slot in its recipe that holds a Spirits-shelf id, so a Boulevardier is Whiskey and Between the Sheets is Brandy. Inside a section, drinks go A to Z. Under the title, one small `muted` line names the sections with their sizes ("Gin 15 · Rum 3 · Whiskey 10 · Without a spirit 3"). Each name in it jumps to its section. That line is the only place a section's size appears.

### 4.6 Next bottle: one list, one rule

The list is ranked by **what adding the bottle really pours**: the full-slot gain, `gains.get(id).length`, so a member of a family counts its family's drinks too. Ties go to the bottle more recipes name (`uses`), then A to Z. An **echo** is dropped: every drink it would add is already claimed by a row above, and recipes rarely name it (`uses` < 3). This is `pickerOrder`'s existing echo rule, so London dry gin never stands next to Gin claiming the same 24 drinks. The row's number is exactly what its Add pours, so the list visibly reads in descending order. The **next line on the screen is row 1 of this list**, which makes it the only answer to "what should I buy?". `bestBottle` becomes `nextBottles(result)[0]`. `shoppingList`/`nextBest` (canonical, read by nothing) and the separate best-bottle card retire.

For the stocked bar: Dry vermouth 10 (Martini, Clarito …), Applejack 8, Vodka 7, Mint 6, Cognac 6, White wine 6, Grenadine 5, Grapefruit 5, Scotch whisky 5, Silver tequila 5.

### 5.8 Smaller things

### 5.9 Families: "rum" against "white rum"

The index's rule stays as it is: a generic expands to its family, and a specific never expands to its generic.

- **Owning White rum** pours drinks that ask for white rum *and* drinks that ask for any rum. With lime and sugar syrup it makes 2: Daiquiri and Barbados Rum Punch.
- **Owning generic Rum** (the person who has "a rum" and does not know which) pours only the any-rum drinks. With lime and sugar syrup it makes 1: Barbados Rum Punch, and no Daiquiri.
- **In the Spirits picker**, the generic heads its family as "Rum" with the small line "Any rum". Once you own any member, the line reads "Any rum · covered by your white rum" and shows no "+N", because ticking it would add nothing (frame 5 shows the same for Whiskey and bourbon). The members below keep their own "+N", for the drinks that need that kind.
- **In the inventory**, both a generic and a member can sit on Spirits ("Rum", "White rum"). Each tile is just what you said you have.
- **Next bottle** lists a member by its full gain (Applejack 8 = 5 applejack drinks + 3 any-brandy drinks), and the echo rule keeps a rarely named member from repeating its family's drinks.
- **Legacy ids** (`vermouth`, `red-vermouth`) are still renamed by the store's `RETIRED` map on load.

## 6. What the numbers do, live

| Number | Where | Recomputed |
|---|---|---|
| "You can make N" | Make row, picker foot, basics foot ("Makes N") | On every change, from `matchOwned(owned)` (memoised on the store's object, as now). It updates the moment a box fills. |
| "+N" per picker row | Pickers and search | Live, as `gainOf(result, id)` for unowned rows and blank for owned ones. Rows never move. |
| "Add X → N more drinks" | Next line | Live: `nextBottles(result)[0]`, skipping ids removed this visit. It is not under a finger when it changes, because changes come from sheets or the toast. |
| "N more, like …" | Next bottle rows | The order is frozen at open; the counts are live. |
| "In N of the M you can make" | Ingredient sheet | Taken when the sheet opens. |
| "N fewer drinks" | Toast | `diffMakeable(before, after).lost.length` (the existing diff). |
| Section sizes | Make list jump line | When the list is pushed. |

## 7. Mapping onto the code

### 7.1 `src/store/bar.ts`: unchanged

The store keeps `owned: Record<string, true>`, `toggle`, `add`, `clear`, `RETIRED` and the guarded persistence. Undo is `toggle(id)` again. The basics sheet calls `add(tickedIds)`. Clear's undo is `add(previousIds)`, held in screen state for as long as the toast lives.

### 7.2 `src/lib/bar.ts`: what is added

```ts
export type Shelf = 'spirits' | 'liqueurs' | 'mixers' | 'fresh' | 'syrups' | 'bitters' | 'other';
export const SHELF_ORDER: readonly Shelf[];               // the order in 4.1
export const SHELF_LABEL: Record<Shelf, string>;          // "Liqueurs & vermouth" …
export const SHELF_EXAMPLE: Record<Shelf, string>;        // the empty shelf's quiet line
export type Family = 'gin' | 'rum' | 'whiskey' | 'brandy' | 'tequila' | 'vodka' | 'other';
// Ingredient gains three fields from the index: shelf, family? (spirits only), aliases: string[]

/** The screen: owned ids by shelf, A to Z (folded). Every shelf, empty ones too. */
export function inventory(owned: Record<string, true>): { shelf: Shelf; items: Ingredient[] }[];
/** A picker's fixed rows: by uses then A to Z; Spirits by family first. Worked out once per shelf. */
export function shelfList(shelf: Shelf): { family?: Family; items: Ingredient[] }[];
/** The id of an owned family member that already satisfies this generic, or null. */
export function coveredBy(id: string, owned: Record<string, true>): string | null;
/** 4.6: full-slot gain, ties to uses then A to Z, echoes dropped. */
export function nextBottles(result: BarResult, skip?: ReadonlySet<string>): { ingredient: Ingredient; matches: readonly Match[] }[];
/** 4.5: the family of the first spirit slot, or 'none'. Cached per recipe. */
export function baseSpirit(drinkId: string): Family | 'none';
```
