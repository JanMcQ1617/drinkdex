## The six cards

Authored cards in `scripts/winedexdata/00-classics.json`, where the made-anywhere style cards already are (Bordeaux Blend, Provence Rosé, White Zinfandel, Orange Wine, Pét-Nat), each in its family's alphabetical place, since that file is sorted by family and then name. `merge-wines.mjs` numbers new cards in file order, so the Dessert two come first:

| Nº | Card | Family (Style filter) | Origin | Strength | Bottles |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|
| 7900 | New World Moscato | Dessert | New World | 7–12% | 13 |
| 7901 | Sweet Red | Dessert | Worldwide | 10–12.5% | 3 |
| 7902 | California Red Blend | Regional Red (Red) | California, USA | 12–16% | 20 |
| 7903 | Red Blend | Regional Red (Red) | Worldwide | 12–14.5% | 5 |
| 7904 | New World Rosé | Rosé | New World | 10–14.5% | 14 |
| 7905 | White Blend | White | Worldwide | 12–13.5% | 2 |

Each carries every field the style cards carry: a description, a strength range, origin, rarity (common, never shown), four tasting notes, a glass, a fun fact, a serve (temperature, glass, how, three pairings) and a composition with Grapes, Region, Vinification and Aging lines.

**The names.** California Red Blend, Red Blend, White Blend and Sweet Red are Jan's words and the trade's. The rosé is **New World Rosé**, Jan's word: it is a California, Australian and South African card, and "Rosé" alone would read as all rosé. The Moscato is **New World Moscato**, not "Moscato": the atlas lists Moscato as a synonym of Muscat Blanc à Petits Grains, so a card of that name reads as a grape card to `wine-brands.mjs` (`styleFacts`), and every bottle under it would point at Muscat Blanc, which [yellow tail]'s Muscat Gordo Blanco (Muscat of Alexandria) is not. Not "Sweet Moscato" either, since Moscato d'Asti is sweet too. Its description names Moscato d'Asti as the wine it is made in the image of; **Moscato d'Asti, Nº 2332, is untouched**. All six names fold to no other card's in any category, no atlas wine's and no grape's (the test checks each), and every character in them is one the fonts already draw.

**The words, and where they come from.** Written in the register of the existing cards and held to App Review 1.4.3: what is in the glass and where it is from, how it is served; nothing about drinking, drinking more, a time or occasion, or being easy to drink. Facts checked for this:

- California Red Blend: the 75% rule (27 CFR 4.23: a US wine may take one grape's name only at 75% or more of it, or name two or more grapes if every grape in it is named with its share), and the grapes on the producers' own pages. Zinfandel and Petite Sirah lead many (the Prisoner, Conundrum, Bogle, Unshackled), not all: Ménage à Trois's is Zinfandel, Merlot and Cabernet, Silk Soft Pinot Noir, Malbec and Petite Sirah, Red Velvet Merlot-led. So the card says "often" and its summary "red grapes in any share". Apothic Inferno is whiskey-barrel finished, Bogle's oak American and French.
- New World Rosé: "blush", credited to the wine writer Jerry Mead in 1976 for a pale Cabernet Sauvignon from Mill Creek Vineyards (Wine Spectator, VinePair); direct press (Bogle, JUSTIN) and saignée. Mostly red grapes, not only: Decoy's has 11% Vermentino, Barefoot's Riesling and DAOU's 14% Sauvignon Blanc, on their producers' pages, so the card says "sometimes with a white … in the blend".
- New World Moscato: Muscat's linalool and geraniol (the Muscat aroma literature; USDA work on Muscat monoterpenes); Muscat Gordo Blanco is Muscat of Alexandria; a light spritz in some, not most: [yellow tail]'s ("light, perky fizz"), Wolf Blass's ("lightly spritzed") and Jacob's Creek's, while Barefoot's and Sutter Home's pages describe a still wine; a dash of red wine for the pink and red (Sutter Home: "a luscious fusion of red and white"), which taste of red berries (Barefoot's red: "red cherries & raspberries"; Sutter Home's pink and red: strawberry).
- Red Blend: Shiraz with Cabernet sold as "claret" in Australia since the late 1800s, and Grange, from the 1950s, mostly Shiraz with a little Cabernet (Jancis Robinson, Wine Australia). South Africa's are Shiraz and the Bordeaux grapes (KWV Roodeberg: Cabernet, Shiraz, Merlot, Petit Verdot, Tannat); Pinotage with the Bordeaux grapes is the Dex's Cape Blend card (Nº 4217), so this card leaves it there. Some are off-dry: 19 Crimes The Banished, which its producer calls "distinctly sweet", is 13.4 g/l of sugar at 13.5% (Vinmonopolet, 2024; "halbtrocken" at Heinemann), so it stays a red blend and the card says "some are left off-dry".
- White Blend: Alsace's Edelzwicker, "noble blend", any of the region's white grapes in any share (Kermit Lynch, wein.plus). The American white blends' grapes are their producers' (Conundrum: Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Semillon, Viognier; Apothic White: Chardonnay, Riesling, Pinot Grigio).
- Sweet Red: Süssreserve ("sweet reserve"), unfermented juice blended back before bottling, at most 15% of the wine under German law (Decanter, the Oxford Companion, Wikipedia's *Sweetness of wine*).
- **Strength ranges are the bottles'**, from the producers' pages where they give one and retail listings where they do not: the Moscatos 7% (Wolf Blass) to 11.8% (Sutter Home Red Moscato); the sweet reds 10.5% (Barefoot) to 12% (Jammy Red Roo, Sutter Home); the rosés 10% (Barefoot, listed at 10% and 10.5%) to 14.1% (DAOU, one listing; 13.7% at K&L); the California blends 12% (Woodbridge, by its producer's 2023 Sessions page; retailers now list 13.5–14%) to 15.9% (Apothic Inferno); the Australian and South African blends 12.5% (The Banished, UK listings) and 13.5% (Big Bold Red; The Banished elsewhere), inside the card's 12–14.5%. A bottle with no strength of its own shows its card's range, and every bottle's own strength is inside it (the test checks).

**Cards written for more than one colour.** `scripts/lib/wine-brands.mjs` reads an authored card's colours from its family, and Dessert means a sweet white; `COLOURS_OF` names the cards written for others, as it does Moscato d'Asti (Sweet White and Sparkling). Sweet Red is filed Dessert, where STYLE files every sweet red, and its words are a sweet red's **alone** (`['Sweet Red']`): a sweet white filed under it would take the sweet white's words, not the red's. New World Moscato's are written for its white, pink and red bottles. Without that, a Red Moscato took STYLE's sweet-red words, dried fig and cocoa at 10–15% and cellar temperature, which are a dessert wine's. Its serve is worded for all three ("orange blossom and peach in the white and red berries in the pink and the red"); its tasting notes are the white's (muscat grape, orange blossom, peach, honey), so `NOTES_OF` gives the five pink and red bottles their own: muscat grape, strawberry, raspberry, cherry (`cardNotes`, read by `merge-wine-brands.mjs`).

**The pour colour.** Dessert pours amber, so a Sweet Red and a Red Moscato would have poured amber and a Pink Moscato too. `liquid.ts` gains "sweet red", "red moscato" and "jammy red roo" in the red rule ([yellow tail]'s Jammy Red Roo, a sweet red whose name does not say so, once sold as Sweet Red Roo) and "pink moscato" in the rosé rule (checked against every drink of build 41: none pours differently).

## The atlas and the filters

**On the map.** Bordeaux Blend and Provence Rosé point at the appellations that define them; these six have none, and the atlas has no row for them (it is the map of appellations, protected names and classic styles; adding six would change the atlas build 41 shipped, and "Red Blend, Australia" is no denomination). So each says why, as the two vermouths do, in `build-wine-atlas-links.mjs`'s `ABSENT`: "A style made across the New World, rather than an appellation wine." and so on. California Red Blend's reads "A style blended from vineyards anywhere in California, rather than the wine of one place on the map.", since "California" on its label is itself a (state-wide) appellation of origin, as its own Region line says. Sweet Red's adds "The sweet reds of named places, Kindzmarauli and Recioto della Valpolicella among them, are on the map under their own names.", because the atlas styles eighteen appellations Sweet Red, and "rather than an appellation wine" alone would read as if none were. Their 57 bottles are on the map nowhere, as every bottle filed under a style made anywhere is (Bordeaux Blend's), and carry their own grapes where the producer names them: 17 do (The Prisoner Red Blend is under Grape: Zinfandel, Petite Sirah, Syrah, Merlot, Malbec and Cabernet Sauvignon).

**Grape.** The cards are in no Grape option. A link of `absent` has no grapes, and a list of "typical grapes" for Red Blend or Sweet Red would be a guess (Bordeaux Blend's come from its appellations, which these have none of). The bottles that name grapes are under them. *Jan's call*: if he wants California Red Blend under Zinfandel and New World Moscato under the two Muscats, the place is a `grapes` list on those two links (the app reads one on any link); the v4.15 test's "only a bottling carries grapes of its own" would then name them.

**Style.** By family: California Red Blend and Red Blend are Red, New World Rosé is Rosé, White Blend White, New World Moscato and Sweet Red Dessert. Each bottle is in its own colour's option (the pink and red Moscatos and the sweet reds Dessert).

**Country and Region.** California Red Blend is "California, USA", in Country USA and Region California, as White Zinfandel and Meritage are. Red Blend, White Blend and Sweet Red are "Worldwide", as the spirit styles made everywhere are (Contemporary Gin, Flavored Vodka), and in no Country or Region option. New World Rosé and New World Moscato are "New World": `dexFacets.ts`'s `PLACE_OF` gained `'New World': []`, so the app reads it as no one country, as it reads "Worldwide", rather than making "New World" a Country option. Each bottle keeps its own country and region (a bottle under a style made anywhere always does): Region California (44; JUSTIN's and DAOU's rosés written "California (Central Coast)" and Bogle's "California (Central Valley)", as the Dex writes sub-regions), South Eastern Australia (10), Coastal Region (1, Mulderbosch) and Western Cape (1, KWV); Wolf Blass's Moscato, whose producer gives its region as "Multi Region", is in Australia and no Region option. No Country or Region option is new, and not one of build 41's 7,885 entries, in any category, moved in or out of any Strength, Style, Grape, Country or Region option (the test runs build 41's own `dexFacets.ts` beside today's).
