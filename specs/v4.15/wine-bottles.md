# v4.15: wine bottlings in the Dex

The Dex's wines were appellations, styles and grapes: "Pauillac", not "Château Latour". This adds the bottles, as a brand layer the way spirits have one (`merge-spirit-brands.mjs`, `-sb`). **3,214 bottlings, Nº 4675 to Nº 7899**, after build 40's highest number (4674). The first run numbered 3,224 bottles 4675–7898; the fact-check (below) retired 11 of those numbers, which stay unused, and gave 7899 to a bottle it renamed. The Dex goes from 4,671 entries to **7,885: 899 cocktails, 1,428 spirits and 5,558 wines** (2,344 wine cards and 3,214 bottlings). No existing card changed its number, name, id, category or any word (below, *Proof*).

Dex titles do not move: they count cocktails and spirits only (specs/v4.15/ranks.md), so a bottle, like any wine, never counts toward a title or against one.

The list to photograph from: `specs/v4.15/wine-bottles-photo-list.csv` (below).

## The source

`scripts/winebranddata/`, one file per part of the wine world, rows in the order the research listed them (house by house, a house's range together). A row is a bottle:

```
{ "n": "Krug Clos du Mesnil", "s": "Le Mesnil-sur-Oger", "c": "France", "region": "Champagne",
  "colour": "Sparkling", "grapes": ["Chardonnay"], "abv": "12.5%", "note": "…", "src": "https://…" }
```

`n` is the producer's spelling. `s` is the wine card it is filed under, by its exact Dex name: an atlas appellation, an authored region or style, or a grape. `colour` is one of the atlas styles (Red, White, Rosé, Sparkling, Sparkling Rosé, Sparkling Red, Sweet White, Sweet Red, Sweet Rosé, Orange, Vin Jaune, Fortified); a sparkling rosé is Sparkling Rosé, never Sparkling. `region`, `grapes`, `abv` and `note` are there when a source gave them (220 rows carry a strength; none carries a note yet). `src` is the page the bottle was confirmed on; it stays in the source and is never written to a card.

## Numbering

New numbers after the highest (4674), in file order and row order within a file, which is what the first run of `merge-wine-brands.mjs` made. Sticky by id like every merge (`scripts/lib/dex-merge.mjs`): a re-run is a no-op, a new row numbers after the Dex's highest. Ids are the name folded and `-wb` (`krugclosdumesnil-wb`), as the spirit layer's are `-sb`, so a renamed row is a new bottling with a new number. A row taken out later does not take its card with it: the merge refuses until the card is removed with `node scripts/remove-drinks.mjs <id> --keep-numbers`, which keeps the phones' records and leaves the gap, so a number is never quietly reused. Without `--keep-numbers` it closes the gap and renumbers every later card, which is what it must not do here; its message says so, and its "still in a source file" guard now reads the brand layers by the id their merges give a row (`-sb`, `-wb`), so it refuses a bottling whose row is still there. The Dex reads a gap as a gap: a jump to a retired number says "There is no Nº 4756. The Dex runs from Nº 0001 to Nº 7899." (`dex.tsx`). No build has shipped a bottling, so no phone holds a retired id.

## How a bottle becomes a card (`scripts/merge-wine-brands.mjs`)

Every field is a fact of the bottle or inherited from the card it is filed under, as the spirits' are.

- **Its own:** name, country, region, colour, grapes, strength (where the row gives one), note.
- **Its card's, where the card's words describe it** (`cardDescribes`, `scripts/lib/wine-brands.mjs`): tasting notes, glass, serve, strength, composition (with the bottle's own grapes, and its own region where it differs from the appellation's). A card's words are written for a colour, and some for one grape: the Côtes de Provence card is a rosé's, Napa Valley's are Cabernet's ("Cabernet Sauvignon, often with Merlot…"), Marlborough's are Sauvignon Blanc's. A card is written for one grape only when its Grapes line says so outright (a grape alone, "only", "alone", or ", with" after it); a list of equals (Champagne, Cava) or a share ("Tempranillo mainly", "Grenache-dominant") is not, so a Garnacha Rioja and Beaucastel keep their card's words.
- **Its colour's, where they do not:** a white Côtes de Provence, a sparkling Vouvray, a Napa Merlot, an Okanagan red take the words the atlas cards are written from (`STYLE`, moved out of `merge-wine-atlas.mjs` into `scripts/lib/wine-styles.mjs` so one table serves both). 2,859 bottles take their card's words, 355 their colour's.
- **Rarity** is always the card's (never shown; wines are never scored).
- **The description** says what is filed where: "A Champagne from France. Made from Pinot Noir, Meunier and Chardonnay." "A white Côtes de Provence from France." "A sparkling rosé Sekt Austria from Austria." "A Shiraz from South Eastern Australia, Australia." "A Shiraz blend from South Australia, Australia. Made from Shiraz and Cabernet Sauvignon." (Grange). A grape card is named as the bottle spells the grape (Primitivo, Shiraz, Pinot Gris); a card's Dex qualifier is dropped in prose ("An Icewine", not "Icewine (Ontario)"), in the fun fact as in the description. The article reads the vowel under an accent: "An Échézeaux", "An Évora", "An Ürziger Würzgarten". The fun fact is "One of 114 Champagne bottlings catalogued here." until a row carries a note.
- **The origin** puts a bottle filed under a place in its card's Region option, the very one, as the app works regions out. `merge-wine-brands.mjs` asks the app's own code rather than copying its rules (`scripts/lib/dex-regions.mjs` transpiles `components/dex/dexFacets.ts`, as `build-share-index.mjs` does `drinkLabels`): the brackets dropped, `REGION_ALIAS` (Niederösterreich is Lower Austria), "Maipo Valley" read as Maipo where Maipo is written, a card named for a region its country writes placed in that region, England read out of "United Kingdom". A bottle takes the first of its own region, its card's origin, and its card's name that lands it beside its card: Château Latour's own "Bordeaux (Left Bank), France" (the Pauillac card is in Bordeaux); a Biondi-Santi's card origin "Montalcino, Italy", where its row says Tuscany; "Cahors, France" for a Cahors, since the app puts the Cahors card in Cahors whatever its origin says. 2,989 keep their own region, 225 take their card's. A bottle of a nationwide name (Vin de France, the IGPs, Sekt Austria) keeps its own region only where some card is in it, and is otherwise in none, as its card is. One filed under a grape or a style keeps its own: "South Eastern Australia" (26), "Buenos Aires" and "East Coast" are Region options of their own, and Kim Crawford Chardonnay's Hawke's Bay is the Hawke's Bay card's. The merge refuses if a bottle cannot be placed, or if the bottlings move any card's own region. The composition's Region line keeps the bottle's own region throughout: a Rioja Cava (Muga's, Faustino's) is in Region Catalonia with the Cava card and says Rioja on its page.

**It refuses**, writing nothing: a style that is not a wine card, a colour the table does not know, a bottle filed under a place in another country, a strength not written "13.5%" or "11.5–12.5%", a key a row may not carry, two rows that fold to one name, a name another card has, a cross-category id collision, any change in another category's size, a bottling card whose row has left the source, a bottle that lands outside its card's Region option, and a card the bottlings move out of its region.

## The map, and the Wine Atlas

`build-wine-atlas-links.mjs` links every bottle from its row: to its one appellation (Château Latour, Pauillac), to its grape when it is filed under a variety ([yellow tail] Shiraz, Syrah), or to nothing when it is filed under a style made anywhere (11: Bordeaux Blend, Orange Wine, Pét-Nat, Provence Rosé, Meritage), since a link to a family would say "10 matching appellations" of one bottle. A bottle's link also carries `grapes`, its own varieties by atlas name, where its row names any the atlas knows (3,091 bottles): the Grape filter reads those instead of everything its appellation permits (Krug Clos du Mesnil is Chardonnay, not Le Mesnil's three). A row's grape is read against its card before the atlas at large (`atlasGrapes`), because a name that is only a synonym means different grapes in different places: "Bonarda" is the atlas's Croatina, Lombardy's, but El Enemigo's, Zuccardi Emma's and Zuccardi Serie A's are Bonarda Argentina, and "Trebbiano" is Trebbiano Abruzzese for Masciarelli, Valentini and Emidio Pepe, not Trebbiano Toscano. So such a name under a grape card whose variety it begins is that variety; else the one grape it begins that is named for the bottle's country (Bonarda Argentina, though the atlas files Santa Rosa's Bonarda as Croatina); else, under a place, the grape the place permits, or the one whose name or synonym it begins (Trebbiano Abruzzese). A grape's own name (Grenache, Pinot Noir) is always that grape. 33 grape names the atlas does not have (Corvinone, Bourboulenc, Counoise, Maturana Blanca, "Field blend"…) stay on the card's Grapes line and are in no Grape option. `AtlasLink` gained the optional `grapes`; `dexFilter`'s `grapesOf` reads it first.

**Bottles do not appear in the Wine Atlas.** The atlas is the map of 2,315 appellations and styles and the 465 grapes behind them, and each row opens the appellation's own card; 3,214 bottles under their appellations would bury it (Champagne alone would hold 114) and would want UI the atlas does not have. A bottle reaches the atlas from its card's **On the map** ("1 matching appellation in the atlas, across 1 country", or its grape's line), and `cardOfAtlasWine` leaves bottlings out, so an atlas row never opens a bottle. If Jan wants an appellation's bottles listed, the place for it is the appellation's Dex card ("27 bottlings in the Dex"), not the atlas.

## The rest of the app

- **Filters.** Colour (Style), Grape, Country and Region, as above; every bottle has one colour and a country, and a bottle filed under a place is in its card's Region option (Region = Montalcino holds the Brunello card and its 16 bottles; the national names, Vin de France and the IGPs, are in none, as their cards are). Proven by `specs/v4.15/wine-bottles.test.mjs`, which runs the app's `dexFacets` and `dexFilter`: and not one existing card moved in or out of any of the 1,042 Style, Grape, Country and Region options.
- **Search** finds every bottle by its name and by producer words ("opus one", "yellow tail"), and by its grapes, through the Dex grid's existing search.
- **Stats, collections, posting, places, tournaments and the share index** read drinks by id or by category and needed nothing: a bottle is a wine, so it is in Dex progress's Wines row and its bar. `docs/c/drinks.json` is regenerated: 7,885 drinks, 533 KB (was 261 KB); `site-dist/c/drinks.json` is that file copied, as `build-site.mjs` copies it, so `build-site.mjs --check` stays in step. **Push it with the release**, or a bottle in a shared collection shows on the web as "A drink added on Sipply".
- **The recommender.** Wines are never scored and hold no taste chip, so nothing moved: every run's picks and matchingCount are as pinned (633, 147, 2311, 437, 185, 239). `classics.json` and `tasteTags.json` were rewritten by `check-recommend.mjs --write` (3,214 new ids each, and the stamp).
- **The pour colour and the glass** read the stored family and glass, which are the families and glasses the Dex already has; `check-artwork` flags nothing.

## The fact-check

Three verifiers sampled the merged layer: 403, 752 and 38 rows, 1,193 in all (about one in four of the bottles once overlaps are counted). **16 rows were wrong, 1.3% of those sampled** (6 of 403, 11 of 752 with EA Tinto found by both, none of the 38), and each was confirmed by a search of its own before anything changed. The highest rates were Iberia (6 of the 16) and the rest of France (3), so every Iberian row was re-checked, and every row of the rest of France read with the doubtful ones searched; a scan of every file for a rosé name under a colour that is not rosé found the sparkling rosés outside the samples. 34 rows changed in all:

- **Taken out, their numbers retired (10):** Mumm Grand Cordon (4756), Mumm's non-vintage brut in its 2016 Ross Lovegrove bottle and by every account the Cordon Rouge cuvée (its two cards were word for word the same), which mumm.com sells as Cordon Rouge again (4755, kept); Domaine Lafage Miraflors Rosé and Centenaire (5697, 5709), IGP Côtes Catalanes in every recent vintage, not AOP Côtes du Roussillon; Casal Garcia Tinto (6442), an IG Lisboa from Aveleda, not a Vinho Verde; and six Vinho Regional Alentejano filed under DOC cards that say "DOC": EA Tinto (6539, under Évora), Monte Velho Red, White and Rosé (6431–6433, Esporão's tech sheet says Regional Alentejano), and Herdade do Peso Vinha do Monte Red and White (6461, 6462, under Vidigueira). The Dex has no card any of them belongs under, so they go as Mateus did (*Cards that would bring the style-gap rows back*, above).
- **Renamed (1):** Domaine du Tariquet Rosé St-Amand is "Domaine du Tariquet St-Amand Le Rosé" (the house's own name for it), Syrah, Marselan and Malbec, not Merlot, Cabernet Franc and Syrah. A new name is a new id: Nº 7899, and 5720 retired.
- **Refiled (7):** Le Serre Nuove dell'Ornellaia under Bolgheri (Bolgheri DOC Rosso, not Toscana IGT); El Coto Verdejo under Rioja Blanco, region Rioja (DOCa Rioja, from Finca Carbonera, not Rueda); Studio by Miraval Rosé under IGP Méditerranée (not Côtes de Provence); Secret de Léoube Rosé under Côtes de Provence (not the La Londe DGC, which is Léoube's top cuvée); Casal Garcia Loureiro, Aveleda Loureiro and Aveleda Alvarinho under Vinho Verde (no source names the Lima or Monção e Melgaço sub-region for them, and Aveleda is in Sousa; Ameal and Niepoort's Dócil are Lima's and stay).
- **Recoloured (12):** sparkling rosés written Sparkling, so they sat in the white-sparkling words: Bründlmayer, Schloss Gobelsburg and Schlumberger's Brut Rosé (6634, 6644, 6675), Nyetimber, Chapel Down, Ridgeview Fitzrovia, Gusbourne and Movia Puro Rosé (6795, 6799, 6803, 6808, 6812), and Barefoot Bubbly, Mumm Napa, Domaine Carneros and Schramsberg Brut Rosé (6972, 7053, 7056, 7057). Each is now Sparkling Rosé and takes its words ("A sparkling rosé Sekt Austria from Austria."). Domaine Carneros keeps its name: Cuvée de la Pompadour is the same wine's other name, and the house makes one Brut Rosé.
- **Grapes and region (4):** Vega Sicilia Valbuena 5° is Tinto Fino and Merlot (no Cabernet Sauvignon, which is Único's); Faustino I Blanco Gran Reserva Viura and Chardonnay; Barón de Chirel Blanco Viura, Garnacha Blanca, Malvasía and Maturana Blanca (Rioja, its own page); Kim Crawford Chardonnay is from Hawke's Bay.

And five code defects, all fixed: the article before an accented vowel (six descriptions read "A Évora", "A Échézeaux", "A Ürziger Würzgarten"); 66 fun facts that named the card with its qualifier ("One of 9 Icewine (Ontario) bottlings"); the origin rule, which kept a bottle's own region whenever any card wrote it, and so left 81 bottles in a different Region option from their card (all 16 Brunellos in Tuscany beside a Montalcino card, 13 Santorinis in Cyclades, 12 Maipo and 8 Colchagua bottles in Central Valley, 8 Cahors and 4 Madiran in Southwest France, and nine multi-region DOCs); the grape read without its card (Argentina's Bonardas under Croatina, Abruzzo's Trebbianos under Trebbiano Toscano); and the refusal message, which pointed at `remove-drinks.mjs` without `--keep-numbers`. ## Proof
